How to Prepare Jollof Rice with Daniel

Jollof rice is a celebrated one-pot dish from West Africa, loved at weddings, birthdays and family dinners across the country and beyond. Long-grain rice is cooked in a rich, spiced sauce of tomatoes, red peppers and onions until every grain is tender and full of flavour. This recipe serves about six people and takes roughly one hour and fifteen minutes from start to finish.

Ingredients

Rice and Sauce Base

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 6 large ripe tomatoes
  • 3 red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 2 Scotch bonnet peppers (ata rodo), adjusted to taste
  • 2 medium onions, divided
  • 1/2 cup vegetable oil
  • 3 tablespoons tomato paste
  • Salt, to taste

Seasoning and Liquid

  • 3 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 teaspoons curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon dried thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 2 stock cubes
  • Salt, to taste

Safety Notes and Warnings

Handle Scotch bonnet peppers carefully. Wear gloves when cutting them and never touch your eyes or face afterwards, as the oils cause painful burning.

Be careful when blending hot ingredients. If any part of the mixture is warm, let it cool first and do not fill the blender more than halfway, because steam can force the lid off and cause burns.

Beware of hot oil. Add the onions and paste slowly to prevent splattering, and keep children away from the stove.

Cook rice and any added meat thoroughly and store it safely. Refrigerate leftovers within one hour of cooking, since cooked rice left at room temperature can grow harmful bacteria.

Preparation Steps

Preparing the Sauce

  1. Rinse the rice in cool water until the water runs mostly clear, then drain and set it aside.
  2. Roughly chop the tomatoes, red bell peppers, Scotch bonnets and one onion, then blend them until smooth.
  3. Pour the blended mixture into a pot and boil over medium heat for 15 to 20 minutes, until most of the water has evaporated and the mixture thickens.
  4. Slice the second onion. Heat the oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat.
  5. Fry the sliced onion for 2 minutes, add the tomato paste, and stir for 5 minutes until it darkens slightly.
  6. Add the reduced pepper mixture and fry for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring often, until the oil rises to the surface and the sauce tastes sweet rather than sour.

Cooking the Rice

  1. Stir in the curry powder, thyme, bay leaves and stock cubes, then add the stock and bring it to a boil.
  2. Add the washed rice and salt, and stir well so the rice is evenly coated in sauce.
  3. Cover the pot tightly, using foil under the lid if necessary, and reduce the heat to low.
  4. Cook for 25 to 30 minutes, stirring gently once or twice, until the rice is tender. Add a splash of hot water if it dries out before it is cooked.
  5. Turn off the heat, let the pot rest covered for 5 minutes, then fluff with a fork and serve hot.

Cooking Terms Explained

Parboiled
Rice that has been partially boiled in its husk before milling, so the grains stay firm and separate when cooked.
Reduce
To simmer a liquid until some of the water evaporates, making it thicker and more concentrated in flavour.
Simmer
To cook gently in liquid just below boiling point, with only small bubbles breaking the surface.
Fry the sauce
To cook the tomato and pepper base in hot oil until it deepens in colour and the oil separates and floats on top.
Fluff
To loosen cooked rice gently with a fork so the grains separate and steam can escape.

Serving Suggestions

Serve the Jollof rice with fried plantain, coleslaw, grilled chicken or fish. Leftovers keep in the refrigerator for up to three days.
